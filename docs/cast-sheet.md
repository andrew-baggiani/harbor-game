# Cast sheet

Six bodies. Three voices with no body. That covers Monday through Friday.

Do not build a face for a voice-only role. Do not give the attackers a tell you can see. Ordinary clothes, ordinary faces, night shift at a harbor logistics company. No tactical costumes, no smirk, no hoodie-in-the-shadows.

Spread age and build across the six. Do not tie skin, accent, gender, or size to who is lying. The courier is friendly. The departing coworker is not angry. The person on the phone sounds helpful.

Monday already has recorded voices for the courier, the fake service-desk caller, the finance coworker, and the narrator. Reuse those voices. This sheet is who needs a body, and which days share one.

## The six bodies

### 1. Dock runner

Reuse: Monday courier. Thursday cafe, jacket instead of the vest, no lines. Same person, different outer layer.

Appears: MON-01, the door. Thursday, seated in the cafe, not part of the decision.

On screen. A few words on Monday. Silent on Thursday.

Age about 28 to 40. Medium build. Not tall-and-menacing, not slight-and-shifty. High-vis vest over a hoodie, work boots, a parcel in both hands, a lanyard with nothing on it. Weight on one foot, like the van is running. Friendly, a little out of breath, in a hurry that feels normal.

What to read: rushed, warm, not scary. The player should want to help.

### 2. Desk coworker

Reuse: Monday finance ask, Tuesday accounts, Wednesday share link and the customer list. One person all three nights. Same cardigan.

Appears: MON-05. TUE-01. WED-01. WED-02.

On screen and voice.

Age about 34 to 48. Average build. Navy polo, charcoal cardigan, badge on a reel, practical shoes. Leans on the desk partition. Tired in a competent way. Hair and presentation are Andrew's call. Do not play them as ditzy or as the office mom or dad.

What to read: friendly, slightly panicked, passing on someone else's urgency. They believe the request. They are not running a con face.

### 3. Contractor

Do not reuse this body as the departing coworker. The player should not learn a face and then distrust it on Friday.

Appears: TUE-02 in the aisle. The badge on the bench in TUE-04 is this person's, face down. They are not standing there for the badge beat.

On screen and voice on Tuesday aisle. Absent for the badge.

Age about 26 to 45. Average build. Polo or a clean coverall, temp badge, a small tool bag, sneakers that have worked. Stands close because the aisle is narrow, not because they are crowding. Ordinary. A bit embarrassed that the login died.

What to read: reasonable, stuck, asking for a favor a busy person would grant. Not slick.

### 4. Last day

Appears: FRI-01, floor 14 aisle, their own laptop.

On screen. One line if the player talks to them. Mostly the laptop closing.

Age about 45 to 58. Average build. Button-down, coat already on, a bag packed, a small drive. Posture is done for the day, not furtive. No clenched jaw, no glance over the shoulder staged for the camera. People having a bad last day look like people having a last day.

What to read: quiet, finished, not a villain. The screen is the tell, not the face.

### 5. At home

Appears: THU-04, the kitchen.

On screen and voice. One line.

Age about 30 to 50. Easy build. Home clothes, bare feet or slippers, a mug. Sits down at the laptop like the table is theirs, because it is. Relaxed. Not a stranger, not a crook.

What to read: too familiar, and entitled to the room. The player likes them. That is the point.

### 6. Night desk

Reuse: lobby presence Monday, the person who takes the badge Tuesday, the person who takes the report Friday. One uniform, three nights. Reception and security are this one job after hours. Do not build two.

Appears: Monday lobby, if a face is needed behind the desk. TUE-04. FRI-01. FRI-02 if someone has to receive the report in person. The phone incident line is not this body.

On screen. Short lines. Calm.

Age about 32 to 50. Medium build. Pressed shirt, radio, no vest full of gear, no sunglasses. Stands square. Does not swagger. Not a bouncer.

What to read: steady. They are the adult in the lobby. Not suspicious of the player. Not warm enough to wave someone in.

## Voices, no body

### 7. Fake service desk

Already recorded for Monday. Do not build a MetaHuman.

Appears: MON-03 only. Do not reuse this voice for the real help desk or the Friday incident line. The player has to be able to hear the difference later.

Voice only.

Age in the voice about 30 to 45. Helpful, unhurried, close to the mic, like they do this all day. Not sinister, not breathless.

What to read: competent. The rush is in the words, not in a villain performance.

### 8. Finance lead

One actor, two reads. Thursday incoming call, then the callback on the known number.

Appears: THU-03. Voice only. The desk coworker does not play this. The player may have just heard that person in the office. The phone has to be someone else.

Voice about 40 to 55. The attack read is pressed, familiar, walking. The callback read is the same person, flat, a little confused, not in a hurry.

What to read: too familiar. They know the payment. They do not have time for you. On the callback, the familiarity drops.

### 9. Real desk

Help desk when the laptop is reported, and the incident line on Friday. Same voice. Not voice 7.

Appears: THU-02, the call you place. FRI-03, the incident number. Voice only.

Age in the voice about 35 to 50. Short sentences. No charm, no joke. A person reading a checklist who is glad you called.

What to read: plain. Relief, not praise.

## Do not build

The narrator is already cast. The barista does not need a unique body. The cafe patron is body 1 without the vest. Screens, phones, and the ransom note are not people.
