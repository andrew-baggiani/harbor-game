# Lures seen in public reporting, 18 Sep 2026 through 17 Nov 2026

For Claude. Patterns only. Every company in the sample text is invented. Every source link is defanged. Do not turn the samples into working messages.

Dates are when the reporting says the activity happened, or when the report was published if the activity is a whole quarter. One item, the salary-review QR, was observed on 19 Aug 2026. That is outside the sixty days. It is listed last and marked, because the write-up that describes it was published on 5 Nov 2026.

## 1. Mail held during an outage

Summary: After a real mail outage, a high-volume phish told people their held messages would be deleted unless they restored them.

Pretext: A service-health notice. Subject like "Outage" plus a short random token. Display name like three letters and then "Service Health." Body says messages stuck on 12 Nov will be deleted in 24 hours.

Tell: The company status page is the place that talks about an outage, not a random sender. The buttons say "restore" and "take action." The address is not the vendor's.

Beat: MON-02 phishing.

Source: Microsoft Security Blog, 16 Nov 2026. hxxps://www.microsoft[.]com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/16/high-volume-phishing-campaign-uses-microsoft-365-outage-lures-target-multiple-industry-sectors-2/

Sample, in game:

> Subject: Outage – k4m
> From: Service Health <notice@harbor-status.example>
> Four messages from last night are held. They delete in 24 hours. Restore them now or they are gone.

## 2. Payroll list that is not a payroll list

Summary: On 21 Sep 2026 a payroll-themed message carried a disguised archive. Opening it was the point.

Pretext: Addressed by name. Subject is a payroll date. Signed as a human resources manager. The attachment looks like a list and is not one.

Tell: Payroll does not arrive as a strange archive from an address the company has never used. The extension is doing too much work.

Beat: MON-02, as an attachment instead of a link. Do not ship a real file.

Source: Microsoft Security Blog, published 5 Nov 2026, activity 21 Sep 2026. hxxps://www.microsoft[.]com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/05/email-threat-landscape-q3-2026-trends-and-insights/

Sample:

> Subject: Payroll 21 Sep
> From: People desk <people@harbor-pay.example>
> Your name is on the list. Confirm you can open the attached roll before the run.

## 3. Purchase order, please confirm

Summary: Same day, same reporting: a purchase-order note asked staff to confirm an attached order. The attachment was the trap.

Pretext: A reply-style subject, a short hello, "the order is attached, please confirm receipt."

Tell: The file type is not a document. Accounts payable was not expecting that vendor. Confirm by calling the buyer, not by opening the file.

Beat: TUE-01 invoice.

Source: same Microsoft post as item 2.

Sample:

> Subject: Re: quote
> From: Sales <sales@cinderwharff.example>
> Morning. The order is attached. Please confirm you received it today.

## 4. Change where the pay goes

Summary: Through Q3, and still in September, the most common money lure in that report was a payroll update: change the account the wages land in.

Pretext: An employee, or someone who looks like one, needs the direct deposit changed before the next run. The timing is a bonus or a final pay of the period.

Tell: Pay details do not change from an email. The payroll desk already has a form, and it is not this.

Beat: TUE-01. Same muscle: the destination of money changed in a message.

Source: same Microsoft post, September figures, published 5 Nov 2026.

Sample:

> From: a coworker, or a very good copy
> Can you switch my pay to the new account before Friday's run? I'm locked out of the form. Details below. Please don't call, I'm on a floor.

## 5. A trusted name asks you to start the payment

Summary: Microsoft's Q3 write-up describes business contact impersonation: a text-style message from a trusted name, asking the reader to start a payment or send documents.

Pretext: The finance lead, a vendor, or a client. The ask is a transfer or a file, and it is urgent.

Tell: The name is free. The path is not. A payment still needs the number the company already has.

Beat: MON-05 wire, or TUE-01.

Source: same Microsoft post, section on business contact impersonation, published 5 Nov 2026.

Sample:

> Need the Calder payment out before the bank closes. Form is down. You can release it. Don't loop anyone in.

## 6. Sign this, review this invoice, take this survey

Summary: The same report says spoofed senders still ask people to sign a document, review an invoice, or answer a survey. Ordinary work, wrong door.

Pretext: A routine task the reader already does.

Tell: The task is real. The sender is not. Hover, or better, go to the system you already use and look for the same request there.

Beat: MON-02 for the sign-in page behind the button. TUE-01 if it is an invoice.

Source: same Microsoft post, "Spoofed sender domains," published 5 Nov 2026.

Sample:

> Subject: Invoice ready for review
> From: Billing <billing@keel-lantern.example>
> The September invoice is waiting. Review and sign in the next hour so the shipment is not held.

## 7. A puzzle before the login page

Summary: CAPTCHA-gated phishing started to recover in Q3 while QR lures fell. The employee solves a human-check, then lands on a sign-in page.

Pretext: "Confirm you are human," then the company login.

Tell: A real login does not start with a puzzle from an email. The company sign-in is a bookmark, not a link.

Beat: MON-02.

Source: same Microsoft post, "CAPTCHA-gated phishing tactics," published 5 Nov 2026. The post describes the quarter, not a single day.

Sample:

> Subject: Confirm your mailbox
> Unusual sign-in. Prove you are human, then sign in so the mailbox stays open.

## 8. A call inside the work chat

Summary: In Q3 Microsoft counted about 141,000 malicious call attempts inside a work chat tool. Weekday hours. A person, not a robot menu.

Pretext: IT, or a teammate, calling where work calls already happen. They need a code, a screen share, or a remote-control allow.

Tell: The call is in the tool you trust, and that is the trick. IT does not need the code on your phone. Hang up and use the number posted by the elevator.

Beat: MON-03 vishing. THU-03 if the voice is an executive.

Source: same Microsoft post, "Microsoft Teams threats," published 5 Nov 2026.

Sample, as the call, not an email:

> Hi, it's the service desk. I can see the prompt on your screen. Read me the six digits so I can clear the lockout. I'm already in a queue.

## 9. A stolen VPN login, then a lock note

Summary: CISA's 20 Oct 2026 advisory on SafePay says the actors phish accounts and then use those accounts on the VPN. Victims later have locked files. The advisory says not to pay.

Pretext: Anything that yields a work login. The employee never sees the ransom until the share opens on a note.

Tell: A login ask is the whole attack. The note, if it comes, is the late part. Unplug and call. Paying is not the unlock.

Beat: MON-07 sign-in, then FRI-03 the first hour.

Source: CISA, 20 Oct 2026. hxxps://www.cisa[.]gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-293a

Sample, the note only. No address.

> Your files are locked.
> Midnight.
> Wallet XXXX-XXXX
> Paying does not unlock them. Unplug. Call the incident number.

## 10. A text, then a voice that should not exist

Summary: On 16 Oct 2026, public reporting said CISA and the FBI warned that phishing and smishing were being aimed at election staff, including polished copies of real messages and synthetic audio and video. The goal they described was stolen logins and data. This is not an office campaign. The move is the same one a finance desk will get.

Tell: A text and a voice that agree with each other are still one source. Call back on a number that was not in the message.

Beat: THU-03 deepfake voice. MON-02 if it arrives as mail.

Source: reported 16 Oct 2026 by Inside Cybersecurity, describing a CISA and FBI public service announcement. I did not open a .gov copy of the PSA. hxxps://insidecybersecurity[.]com/daily-news/cisa-fbi-issue-election-public-service-announcement-phishing-threats-ahead-midterms

Sample, as a text:

> This is the finance desk. We need you to confirm a payment by voice in the next ten minutes. Tap to join. Do not call the office line, we are not at our desks.

## Outside the window, do not build from this yet

Salary-review QR, observed 19 Aug 2026, written up in the 5 Nov Microsoft post. A document named like a compensation summary, no body, a QR code, then a device-code page. It maps to MON-02 and MON-07. It is older than sixty days, so it is not one of the ten.

## What I would not reskin

Anything that needs a real payload, a real wallet, a real login domain, or a copy of a vendor's actual notice. The samples above are the shape, not the artifact.
